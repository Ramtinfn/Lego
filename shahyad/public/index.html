A tower the people kept

برجی که مردم آن را نگه داشتند

1966 — A 24-year-old's drawing

When Iran announced a national competition for a monument to stand at the gateway of its capital, the winning drawing came from Hossein Amanat — an architecture graduate just twenty-four years old. His idea was audacious: fold twenty-five centuries of Iranian building into a single silhouette.

Look up through the main vault and you see it: the great elliptical arch of ancient Ctesiphon carrying a pointed Islamic arch above it, the two bridged by geometry drawn from Achaemenid Persepolis — and between them, ribbons of turquoise tile, the colour of every dome from Isfahan to Mashhad.

1971 — Shahyad

Completed in 1971, clad in thousands of hand-cut blocks of white marble from the quarries near Isfahan, it was named Shahyad — "memorial of the kings" — and opened as the ceremonial gate of Tehran. Landing at Mehrabad, it is the first thing a traveller sees: the city introduces itself with this tower.

1979 — Azadi

Then history walked through it. In 1979 the square filled with more than a million people, and the tower was renamed Azadi — "Freedom". It has kept both names ever since, the way a family keeps both a birth name and the name everyone actually uses.

Every era since has gathered beneath it — celebrations, mournings, protests, new year crowds. Governments change; the tower stays. It belongs to no decade and to everyone, which is why Iranians abroad keep its picture on their walls: it is the city itself, distilled to one shape.

Whoever you are, wherever you stand — the tower is where Iran meets itself.

Questions

Is this an official LEGO® set?

No. This is an independent fan design. It is not made, sponsored or endorsed by the LEGO Group. The compatible edition uses premium bricks from other brands; the genuine edition is assembled by us from genuine, new LEGO® elements purchased at retail.

Are building instructions included?

Yes — every set comes with step-by-step building instructions. The pieces arrive new but not pre-sorted, so set aside a little sorting time before you start — most builders find it part of the ritual.

Why doesn't the square include the trees shown in the pictures?

The tree element is a classic piece that left production in 2013, so supply is limited and inconsistent. Rather than delay or inflate the price of every set, the current edition ships without the eight trees and their trunk bricks. The square is complete and displays beautifully without them, and they can be added later without touching the rest of the build.

When will my order arrive?

Within 35 calendar days of order confirmation for addresses in Australia. International orders take longer, depending on international transfer times and customs in your country.

What if a piece is missing or damaged?
